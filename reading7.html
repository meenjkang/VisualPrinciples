Sacasas starts their article by quoting Om Malik’s claim on “Technology and the Moral Dimension” about how the tech industry has failed to account for the ethical and emotional consequences of its innovations. The so-called Silicon Valley culture created by big tech companies like Uber and Google is heavily driven by data, speed, and growth, and ethical values such as empathy, responsibility, and trust are often ignored. He urges for these big companies to add a moral dimension to their products, especially in areas such as data privacy, transparency, and user trust. Sacasas reframes Malik’s claim by challenging the assumption Malik makes about technology lacking a moral dimension. Sacasas says that technology already carries ethical weight through its design and process of use that shape human behavior. For Sacasas, the real problem is not whether ethics is present or not but to recognize the depth of the ethical effects that are already present. Since the usage of AI as supportive tools becomes more prevalent in modern technology, some questions related to the ethics behind AI came to my mind- If AI programs play part in shaping how I make decisions, to what extent am I responsible for the credibility, transparency, in terms of the outcomes they produce? I agreed that the responsibilities of ethics is not only on the deployment of the tech product themselves but also on the users and how they interact/ decide to use that technology.
