What is a ritual, practice, or routine in your life that is important for your psychological wellbeing and/or fulfillment?


Listening to music, especially on vinyl, has become a valuable ritual in my life that grounds me. It began with the orange sleeve of Getz/Gilberto album that I was immediately drawn to in a vinyl store in Seoul. Since then, I collect records following a personal rule: I must love every track on the list. Each album I own carries a fragment of my life. The Goldberg Variations by Bach reminds me of long afternoons drawing with my twin brother, and All Eyez On Me by 2Pac recalls the intensity of 10th grade finals. My record collection holds memories and the energy of different moments. What I value most about my vinyl collection is the act of listening. Playing a vinyl on a record player requires a lot of intention; selecting a side to play, lowering the needle onto the groove, and not being able to skip through songs as I could do on Spotify. It asks me to slow down, and that stillness changes how I experience time. What makes this ritual valuable is the way it enforces presence. Through this process, I learn that human fulfillment is not necessarily about maximizing choice or efficiency. In a society that prioritizes speed and efficiency, there is value in practices that slow us down and limit our options. Fulfillment, then, may come less from doing more and more from learning how to remain.
