In this article, Webb introduces two elements of technology: cyborgs are wearable technology that enhance the human adaptation, and rooms are computed environments that people can enter and leave. While cyborg technologies dominate today by turning the human into the “third device,” Webb argues through examples like Dynamic land and Put-that-there that Room-scale technology is more socially and ethically responsible because it acknowledges that we live and think together in physical space. From my perspective, room-scale computing pushes designers to think about who has access and power in a space rather than focusing only on individuals. In this way, room-scale computing pushes back against systems that profit from collecting personal data and instead treats technology as a shared public and cultural resource rather than a personal tool.
