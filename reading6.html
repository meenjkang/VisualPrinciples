Weiser and Brown start by tracing back the three phases in which technology's relationship with humanity evolved in waves: from mainframes where people shared one computer, to ubiquitous computing where many computers share the individual users. With the increased usage of such technology, they focus on calm technology, which is a kind of technology that gives info that is needed without demanding the user’s attention by distinguishing the ‘center’ of attention and the periphery. Here they state that good technology informs the users through periphery when needed. I deeply agree with this idea in terms of the web communicating with users. However, the concept of a well functioning periphery seems impossible in reality, where many people report feeling less located and aware despite carrying more information-gathering technology than ever. Instead of calm tech, apps and webs are mostly designed to grab user’s attention with notifications and ‘doomscrolling’. Considering this article was written in the 90s, I find it fascinating though for how the authors predicted the network of technology would be in the future (UC phase).
