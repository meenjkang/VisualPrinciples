Interview

What does it mean to use a website?

This interview investigates how users engage with websites as structured yet dynamic environments, focusing on perception, behavioral patterns, and the balance between control and guidance in digital interaction. My inquiry is based on the reading My Website is a Shifting House by Laurel Schwulst, which reframes the website as an evolving, spatial construct rather than a fixed interface. By positioning the website as something fluid and experiential, the text challenges conventional assumptions about usability and authorship. Building on this perspective, my interview attempts to explore how contemporary users navigate, interpret, and respond to these designed environments.

Interviewee

Emily Eunseo Kim

Industrial Design Major at Rhode Island School of Design
(Work experience in UX Design)

Transcript
Q1

When interacting with a website, is the user an active decision-maker or a responder following pre-structured pathways?

Emily

I’d like to believe I’m an active decision-maker, but realistically, I think most of the time I just respond to what’s already been structured for me. From the hierarchy of information—how the website is laid out—to subtle details like color or motion, those choices guide my attention in very specific ways. In that moment, I usually just follow what feels natural. Sometimes I even feel like that natural intuition is something I learned over time through repeated exposure to similar website structures and patterns.

Q2

To what extent do you think your expectations of websites are shaped by repeated exposure to standardized design patterns?

Emily

Oh, I think they’re shaped almost entirely by repetition. As a designer myself, there are certain things I include without even consciously thinking about them, like navigation bars, how links behave, or page transitions. And even as a user, I feel like I expect the same conventions when entering websites. It just feels right to adhere to these standardized patterns, whether I consciously notice them or not. I think those expectations are really learned habits formed by being exposed to so many of them.

Q3

How do websites influence your perception of time during interaction?

Emily

Websites can really distort my sense of time depending on how they’re structured. If everything flows continuously, I tend to lose track of time because there’s no clear stopping point. But if a website requires more deliberate actions, like clicking through different sections or processing unfamiliar layouts, I become much more aware of time. Honestly, I become frustrated when using the latter because I have to actively think about what I’m doing.

Q4

How do you balance the tension between efficiency and experience when interacting with a website?

Emily

I think it depends on my goal. If I’m trying to accomplish something specific, like finding information, I prioritize efficiency. I want everything to be clear and fast. But if I don’t have a specific goal, and especially as a design major, I’m more open to experience. In those cases, I actually appreciate when a website takes more time or requires more exploration because it feels more engaging. So I don’t think one is always more important than the other—it really depends on the context.

Q5

To what extent do you believe users are responsible for how they engage with websites, versus designers shaping that engagement?

Emily

I think designers have a stronger influence because they’re the ones creating the structure and guiding interaction. Most users don’t actively question how a website works; they just follow what feels natural within that framework. But at the same time, I feel like users do have some responsibility in how much attention they give or how critically they engage. Overall, though, I think interaction is mostly shaped by design, where users respond rather than actively control the experience.

Q6

Do you think websites can communicate a sense of authorship or personality?

Emily

Yes, I do think websites can communicate a sense of authorship or personality, particularly when they diverge from standardized patterns and conventions. When a website incorporates distinctive interactions, intentional visual choices, or even minor irregularities, it begins to convey a more individualized presence. These elements signal that the site is the result of deliberate creative decisions rather than a purely formulaic process. I try to make the experience feel more intentional and less generic, and guide the viewer to perceive a clearer sense of the meaning behind my work.

Conclusion

Closing Insight

My interview with Emily reveals that user interaction with websites is largely shaped by learned behaviors and embedded design conventions, often operating at a subconscious level. While users may perceive themselves as active decision-makers, their actions are frequently guided by pre-structured pathways established through interface design. At the same time, there remains a sensitivity to atmosphere, authorship, and experience, particularly when websites deviate from standardized norms. This tension reflects a central idea from Laurel’s text: websites are not neutral tools, but constructed environments that continuously influence how users move, think, and perceive.
