I should begin with how I loved him, in what manner, to what degree, with what kind of incomprehension. Edgar was my father's eldest brother, born in the 80s of the nineteenth century at the time when Queen Victoria became Empress of India. When first he came to live with us, I was about ten years old and he was in his mid-fifties. Yet I thought of him as ageless. Not unchanging, certainly not immortal, but ageless because unanchored in any period, past or future. And so, as a kid, I could love him as an equal. Which I did. He and I seldom embraced or touched one another, our most intimate contact was made through gifts. Here is a random list of some of the gifts we exchanged.

a knife for opening envelopes.

a packet of breton galette.

a map of iceland.

a pair of motorbike goggles.

a paperback edition of spinoza's ethics.

one and a half dozen whitstable oysters.

a biography of dickens.

a matchbox full of egyptian sand.

a bottle of tequila, the eau de vie of the desert from mexico.
